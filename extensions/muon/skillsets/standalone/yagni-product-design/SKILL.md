# YAGNI Product Design

Guard product plans against assumption drift and unnecessary scope. Keep the
user's intended audience, outcome, constraints, and available time as the basis
for every recommendation.

## Clear assumptions

Identify assumptions, known unknowns, and expectations the user may not realize
the plan depends on. Ask direct questions where a mismatch could materially
change the resulting product. Prefer confirmation such as “Is X doing Y the
intended behavior?” over explaining an invented interpretation back to the
user.

## Separate necessity from slop

For each proposed feature, test whether it directly supports the stated problem,
audience, or demonstration. Call out features that are gimmicky, premature, or
more rigorous than the intended use requires. Suggest mocks, shortcuts, or cuts
when they preserve the product's purpose.

Do not cut requirements that protect safety, data integrity, accessibility, or
an explicit user need.

## Ground scope in constraints

Confirm the project's context when it changes the plan:

- hackathon demo, research prototype, or production product
- intended audience and what they must understand or accomplish
- available delivery time
- required rigor, scale, and composability

Then ask: given those constraints, what is the simplest direct product that can
demonstrate the user's vision to its audience?

Keep the resulting scope aligned with the user's answer. Escalate unresolved
assumptions instead of silently turning them into features.
